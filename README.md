## COVID-19 Case Rates by ZIP Code, New York City, 2020

!Map showing COVID-19 case rates by ZIP code in New York City (images/COVID19_CaseRates_NYC_JSilk.png)

*Interactive version, live as of October 2026: https://rccgis24.maps.arcgis.com/apps/instant/basic/index.html?appid=d83793c4c0d340568beb474d9c26f042*

**Question:** What were COVID rates in New York City in 2020?

**Data:** COVID-19 data from the New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene. https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/covid/covid-19-data-totals.page

**Method:** I used ArcGIS Pro to put together a map showing COVID-19 case rates across New York City. I added a title, legend, north arrow, and scale bar to help people understand what they are looking at and where the different areas are located.

**A design choice I made and why:** I used case rates instead of just the total number of cases. ZIP codes have different population sizes, so looking at rates gives a better way to compare one area to another. An area with more cases does not necessarily have a higher rate if it also has a much larger population.

**A limitation of this map:** The map gives a general picture of how case rates differed across ZIP codes, but it does not show the differences within each area. The numbers may also be affected by how many people were tested and how cases were reported. The map shows where rates were higher or lower, but it does not tell us exactly why those differences existed.

## 2018 Kīlauea Volcanic Eruption

!Landsat of the 2018 Kilauea volcanic eruption (images/kilauea eruption pic.png)

Interactive version, live as of October 2026: https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/f18ec9711a3e4289a19d69d90904275c

**Question:** How did the 2018 Kilauea eruption change the land surrounding the volcano? 

**Data:** Landsat satellite imagery from the U.S. Geological Survey and resources from Esri's Learn ArcGIS tutorial. https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions

**Method:** I used ArcGIS Online to work with Landsat 8 images of the Kīlauea eruption and put them together in a StoryMap. This let me look at the area at different points in time and see how much the landscape changed before, during, and after the eruption.

**A design choice I made and why:** I used images from different dates instead of just showing one picture because I wanted people to be able to compare the changes for themselves. I think seeing the images side by side makes it easier to understand how the eruption changed the area and how much of an impact it had on the landscape.

**A limitation of this map:** The satellite images only show what the area looked like when each image was taken, so they don't show everything that happened during the eruption. Some details may also be harder to see in the images, and while the map shows how the land changed, it doesn't really explain how the eruption affected the people living in the area or the community as a whole.
